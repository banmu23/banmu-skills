# Visual Modes

Choose one visual mode for the full carousel. Do not mix modes page by page.

## 1. Personal-IP Doodle

Use for hand-drawn, doodle, sketchbook, whiteboard, handwritten, personal-IP, strange-but-clean, or lively visual requests.

### Visual DNA

- Pure clean white background.
- Black marker and ink line art with slight natural wobble.
- Black and white occupy about 80%-90% of the image.
- Use vivid red-orange for the main action, result, underline, or path.
- Use cobalt blue only for secondary notes, feedback, or a small counterpoint.
- Use large irregular handwritten Chinese, uneven baselines, loose underlines, circles, arrows, and margin notes.
- Keep at least 30%-35% quiet white space when the page allows it.
- Use one physical action or metaphor per page.
- Keep visible Chinese short. Put detailed explanation in the publish body.

### Personal IP

Treat the character as a variable supplied by the account owner.

Preserve only the visible anchors provided by the caller:

- face shape
- hair silhouette
- glasses or other identifying accessory
- facial hair if present
- default clothing color and shape
- mature personality and expression

Transform the portrait into a hand-drawn personal IP. Do not paste the photo into every page.

The IP must perform the core action: pull, crank, connect, sort, catch, feed, repair, press, carry, open, or assemble. Do not use it as a decorative corner portrait.

For a narrative carousel, the IP may appear on inner pages. Vary the action and expression while preserving identity anchors.

If no portrait is supplied:

- ask only if identity materially affects the result;
- otherwise use a neutral mature operator character or make a portrait-free doodle system;
- never borrow another account owner's portrait.

### Avoid

- beige or vintage paper
- paper texture, gradients, lighting, shadows, 3D
- corporate rounded cards and strict grids
- PPT infographics or formal flowcharts
- realistic UI screenshots
- cute mascot, children's illustration, anime, sticker portrait
- crowded wire bundles, dense crosshatching, or mechanical detail
- repeated composition on every page
- a cover-only visual pasted mechanically onto the inner pages

### Prompt Skeleton

```text
Create page {page} of a 3:4 portrait Chinese carousel in a mature hand-drawn personal-IP doodle style.

Pure white background. Dominant black marker line art with natural wobble. Sparse vivid red-orange for the main action and a tiny cobalt-blue accent. Large irregular handwritten Chinese, loose underlines, circles and arrows. Lots of white space. No beige, texture, shadows, vector art, corporate cards, PPT or flowchart.

Personal IP: {portrait anchors}. The character actively {physical action} using {one strange low-tech object} to express {one core idea}.

Exact text only:
{short lines}

No other text, logo, QR code, price or watermark. Exact 3:4 portrait.
```

## 2. Brand-Adaptive

Use when the caller provides a different approved style or asks for editorial, collage, checklist, premium poster, table, sticky-note, or another brand-led treatment.

Infer or ask for:

- palette
- background and texture
- font personality
- information density
- IP placement
- premium vs playful balance
- reference images and approved prior posts

Use the portrait on the cover only by default. Inner pages may use tables, comparisons, sticky notes, timelines, split panels, or scene cards according to content.

## 3. Mode Selection

Priority:

1. The caller's explicit visual request.
2. A confirmed approved reference from the account owner.
3. The account owner's stored brand rules.
4. Personal-IP Doodle for requests that emphasize hand-drawn personality.
5. Brand-Adaptive as the general fallback.

If the caller says the previous version was too corporate, regular, template-like, or color-block heavy, switch to Personal-IP Doodle rather than merely adding a handwritten font to the same grid.

## 4. Customer Privacy

The transferable asset is the visual grammar, not the maintainer's identity.

Customer packages must not contain:

- another owner's portrait
- another owner's name or signature character name
- private local paths
- internal products, clients, cases, or business data

Customer mode uses the customer's portrait and brand inputs. When they are missing, deliver a neutral version and a short list of the minimum assets needed for stronger identity consistency.
